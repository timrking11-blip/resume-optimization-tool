        Where your data goes

        Nowhere, unless you send it. Your record lives in this browser's storage. A backup is a file you download. When you press a button that asks Claude (match questions, converting an answer, polishing a line, the summary), the relevant text goes to Claude on your own Claude account, after you allow it once per visit; Claude's replies are checked here against your own words before anything prints.

        What the page never does: it doesn't invent a number, employer, title or tool; it doesn't print a fact whose evidence is in conflict; it doesn't post, email or share anything; it doesn't keep analytics.

        What Claude never sees: your backup file, your phone number and email (they print on the sheet but are not part of any question), or postings you didn't match while Claude was on.

        How it works

        Most resume tools generate. This one retrieves and asks. Your record is a small evidence base: what you did, in your words, with the numbers you stated. A posting is turned into weighted requirements. The draft is the subset of your record that proves them best, printed in a plain template a recruiter's software reads without trouble.

        Where the draft proves a requirement weakly, you get a question, not a made-up line. Your answer becomes an entry only after the page checks that every number in it appears in what you wrote, and after you approve it. That is the whole design: the model drafts, you own the facts and the signature.

        • Start with: one current resume's worth of roles and bullets, and one posting. Ten minutes.
        • Better with: the accomplishments from two or three older resumes and your LinkedIn summary. In testing, that reached roughly ninety per cent of what a full record finds; past four documents the gain is small.
        • Built for: go-to-market, sales, marketing and revenue roles. The skill list is tuned to those postings.

        A longer guide, with what to bring and why, is linked from here once it is published.

        The four tiers

        Evidence is what happened. Derived knowledge is what the page infers, with a confidence. Generation is what gets written, traced to claims. Feedback is what you did with it. Nothing flows backward without a step you can see.
