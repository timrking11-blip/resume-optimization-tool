Career Companion Hub · the guide

What to bring, what the checks mean, and where your data goes

Career Companion Hub tailors a resume to a job posting from your own record, asks you targeted questions instead of inventing lines, and lets you download Word and PDF files in which every sentence traces back to something you actually said. This page explains how to get the most out of it.

Who it is for

People applying for go-to-market, sales, marketing, revenue operations and partnerships roles. The skill vocabulary the page reads postings with (94 skills in 14 families, from forecasting and lead routing to retail media and lifecycle email) is tuned to those jobs. Other fields will still match on the words they share, but the questions will be less sharp.

It suits you if you would rather answer five pointed questions than let a tool guess, and if you want to sign your name to every line.

What to bring

Upload your current resume (Word, PDF or text) or paste its text, review what the page found, and add it. Ten minutes is enough to start; the more of your history you add, the better the match.

+ two older resumes and your LinkedIn export (Save to PDF from your profile)20 minAround nine tenths of a full record's coverage. Older resumes hold the numbers you have since dropped; those numbers are exactly what postings ask for. Where two documents disagree, the page asks you which is right.

Paste accomplishments one per line, in the words you used at the time. The page keeps your line as evidence, tidies the capitalization and period for printing, tags the skills it proves and notes the numbers it states. A line with a number is worth two without.

How a session goes

  1. Match. Paste a posting. The page turns it into weighted requirements and scores how much of it your record already proves. The draft on the right is the subset of your entries that proves the most, in a plain template a recruiter's software reads without trouble.
  2. Ask. You get up to five questions aimed at the requirements the draft proves weakly, or tools the posting names that your record lacks. Answer with specifics: scope, numbers, outcome. Skip any you like.
  3. Verify. Each answer becomes one to three entries. Before an entry can print, every number in it must appear in your answer. You choose which entries to use and which role each goes under.
  4. Download. Word or PDF, in the neutral template. Downloading also adds the entries you approved to your record, with your answer as their evidence, so the next posting starts stronger.

With Claude allowed on the page (it asks once per visit and runs on your own Claude account), the questions are written for the posting, your answers are turned into resume wording, a Polish button tightens any line, and an optional summary paragraph is written only from the facts on the draft and checked against them. Without Claude, everything still works: the questions come from the gap analysis and each line of your answer becomes an entry as typed.

How the match is scored

The posting is read for the skills it asks for; each gets a weight from how strongly the posting stresses it. The match is the share of that weight already proven by the entries printed under your roles. A skill counts once: the first entry that proves it earns the full weight, and a second entry for the same skill changes nothing. The Skills and Tools lines don't count. An answer therefore moves the match only when it becomes a printed entry that proves a skill nothing proved yet. The page shows where the match can grow, as criteria in the posting's own words, and after the final draft it shows what each answer did: a new entry that proved a skill, an enrichment of an existing entry (better wording, same match), a tool added to the Tools line (no change to the match), or an entry held back because a number was not in your answer.

The readiness checks

The meter under Your evidence tracks six things. You can match a posting before all six pass; they tell you what would sharpen the result.

  • 1
    Your name and email are entered.They print on the sheet. They are never part of a question to Claude.
  • 2
    Every role has a title, employer and start date.Dates order the resume and decide which roles count as recent.
  • 3
    Each recent role has at least three accomplishments.Recent means ended within six years. Fewer than three and the draft cannot choose; it can only print what there is.
  • 4
    At least half of your accomplishments state a number.Numbers are what a posting's "proven", "improved" and "grew" are asking for, and they are the facts the page can check.
  • 5
    Three or more skills and three or more tools listed.They print as the Skills and Tools lines and are ordered by the posting each time.
  • 6
    No facts in conflict.When two statements about the same fact disagree, that fact is held back from printing until you settle it. A number in dispute never prints.

How your record is organized

Everything the page knows sits in four tiers, and nothing moves up a tier without a step you can see.

Tier 1 · EvidenceWhat you saidVerbatim lines: what you typed, pasted or answered, each with a source and a date.
Tier 2 · DerivedClaims with a confidenceFacts drawn from the evidence: titles, dates, accomplishments, the numbers each one states. Your own statement counts as verified.
Tier 3 · GenerationThe draftEvery printed line names the claims behind it. Claude's wording is checked against them before it prints; Claude's words never become evidence.
Tier 4 · FeedbackWhat you did with itKeep, drop and edit clicks, and the outcome of each application. Recorded and reported, never used to rewrite your facts.

Browser storage and backups

The page has no server. Your record lives in this browser's storage for this page, which means:

  • Another browser, another device, a private window or cleared site data will show an empty page.
  • Download a backup after each session (Backup & privacy). It is one JSON file with your whole record: evidence, entries, postings, answers and outcomes. Load it on any device to continue.
  • Storage is limited to about 5 MB. The meter shows what you use; a full record with a dozen postings is well under 1 MB.
  • Delete everything removes the record from this browser. Type delete to enable it. Your backups are unaffected.

Where your data goes

Nowhere, unless you send it. Nothing is posted, emailed, shared or logged, and there are no analytics.

When you press a button that asks Claude (matching with Claude on, converting an answer, Polish, the summary), the relevant text goes to Claude on your Claude account: the posting, your accomplishments and answers, your role titles, employers and dates. Your phone number, email address and backup file are never included. You allow this once per visit, and you can decline: the page keeps working with keyword matching and your answers as typed.

What the page never does

  • Invent an employer, title, date, number, tool or outcome.
  • Print a number that is not in your evidence or your answer, or a fact whose evidence is in conflict.
  • Treat Claude's wording as a fact about you. Only your words are evidence.
  • Rank your history by what "usually works". Keep and drop clicks adjust ordering for you alone, in your browser.

Questions people ask

Uploads?
Word, PDF and text files, or pasted text. The document's lines are stored word for word; Claude (or, without Claude, a layout reader) points at lines for roles, entries, education, skills and tools, and nothing is added until you approve it in the review. Scanned PDFs without a text layer need pasting.
Templates?
One, deliberately plain: single column, standard headings, Calibri, real Word bullets, no tables or text boxes. It is built to be read correctly by applicant tracking systems.
Why five?
Because a posting rarely has more than five requirements your record proves weakly, and because answering well takes attention. You can ask for quick questions from the gap analysis instead at any time.
Editing?
Yes, click any line on the sheet. An edit to a line built from a claim is checked the same way an answer is: numbers you add must already be in your evidence.
Example?
A fictional record you can load into an empty page to see the whole flow before entering your own. Delete everything before entering yours.
